Riverbend Community Health · staff portal rebuild · design review

Seven visual directions for the staff portal — which one would you trust with a chart?

This is a rebuild of the portal that front-desk staff, clinicians, schedulers and records clerks use. Patients never log into it. Everything below shows the same screen seven different ways: the patient identity strip, an allergy banner, a records-request queue, and one form field. Only the colour, type and spacing change.

What I'd like your read on

  1. Does the allergy banner read as urgent — without becoming noise? It's the one red thing on the screen by design. Too quiet is dangerous; too loud and it gets tuned out by 10am.
  2. Can you decide from the status column at a glance, without reading it? This is the one that changed since you last saw the page: status is now a chip with a shape in it, not coloured words. Judge whether you can spot every Pending in a queue at arm's length — and whether the colours match what you'd expect, and whether “Not answered” reads as different from “declined”, which are genuinely different things in records work.
  3. Row height: which would you actually want on a shared workstation? Use the Dense / Comfortable switch below. Dense fits about 8 rows where Comfortable fits 6. Dense sees more at once; Comfortable is harder to misclick.
  4. Is name + date of birth + MRN the right set, in that order, to confirm you're on the right patient? It stays pinned at the top of every screen. If you'd reorder it or want something else in it, that's useful to know now.
  5. Which of these looks like a clinical tool, and which looks like a marketing site? Blunt answers welcome. Direction G is dark — worth saying whether you'd ever want that in an exam room.

On a phone, judge the colour, type and urgency — not the layout. These are desktop screens shrunk down; on a real workstation they're about three times this wide. The queue tables slide sideways with a finger. All the patient details are invented test data.

These switches change every specimen at once

Status was coloured text before — one weak signal doing all the work. Now the three states are deliberately unequal, so the column has one thing to look for instead of three: Pending is a solid block of the attention colour because it is the only one that needs a person, Released is a quiet tint, and Not answered is a dashed outline because nothing has come back yet. Each also carries a shape — ● pending, ✓ released, ○ not answered — which survives greyscale and every form of colour blindness. Flip between the two and say whether the chips earn their weight.

Notes

  • Real proposals: colours, contrast ratios, type sizes, row heights, border weights, focus rings. Every contrast ratio on this page is calculated by the page itself, against that direction's own background.
  • Not a proposal: the arrangement of the screen. That was settled earlier and is reused here only so the colours sit on real content.
  • Deliberately unfinished everywhere: a dark counterpart for each palette, loading and empty states, and the wording review that makes sure no screen talks to staff as though they were the patient.
  • Status chips are derived, not drawn: every chip is built from that direction's own attention/success/neutral colour by one rule — Pending fills solid, Released tints the surface, Not answered stays unfilled — and then the text is darkened (lightened, on the dark direction) until it clears 7:1 against whatever it ends up sitting on, the zebra stripe included. The ratio printed under each queue is measured on the chip you're looking at, so a direction can't pass by being described as passing.
  • Contrast floor: body text at 7:1 or better, secondary text and accents at 4.5:1 or better. A red FAIL badge means that pairing can't ship as text — three colours in the current live portal fail it, which is a finding, not a rendering fault.
